A community conversation about revenue, infrastructure, education, and quality of life in Stafford, Texas.

Why this conversation matters now

Stafford is moving toward an update of its 25-year Comprehensive Plan. A vision for land use, infrastructure, housing, transportation, and quality of life should also include a realistic fiscal sustainability and revenue modernization framework.

Official Context:
The City of Stafford has issued RFP 2026-001 for a Comprehensive Plan Update and Zoning Ordinance rewrite. Proposals are due May 22, 2026. This initiative seeks to ensure the final plan includes a serious, funded component for revenue modernization.

This initiative asks citizens and local leaders to support including that study in the Comprehensive Plan process to ensure our long-range vision is connected to a realistic funding model.

Can Stafford modernize its public revenue structure in a way that protects taxpayers, strengthens infrastructure, supports education, preserves restricted funds, and improves quality of life for the next generation?

The One Taxpayer Principle

Government may divide funds into separate categories, but residents and businesses experience the cost as one community. School taxes, city revenue, sales tax, public works, fees, special districts, infrastructure, and school quality all affect the same taxpayer.

Every major public finance decision should ask: What is the total impact on the Stafford taxpayer, and what value is the taxpayer receiving in return?

The Four-Part Framework

The One Taxpayer, One Stafford, One Future concept is built around four connected areas of study proposed for the Comprehensive Plan Update:

1. Sales Tax & Restricted Funds

Review allocations of local sales tax (City, MST, and SEDC), evaluate voter intent, and study lawful realignment options to modernize funding for the next 25 years.

2. Public Works & Infrastructure

Evaluate dedicated funding models for drainage, streets, sidewalks, and stormwater to address backlogs and sustain core quality-of-life obligations.

3. Education & Youth Partnership

Study lawful ways for the City, SMSD, and SEDC to partner on workforce readiness, student success, joint-use facilities, and family quality-of-life investments.

4. Total Taxpayer Burden

Model the impact of property tax, VATRE, and recapture to determine if a restructured model could reduce the total burden while remaining legally defensible.

What this IS

  • A call for a neutral, professionally guided feasibility study.
  • A civic conversation for Stafford's next generation.
  • A taxpayer-centered planning model.
  • A proposed component of the Comprehensive Plan Update.
  • A request for factual revenue mapping and legal review.

What this IS NOT

  • Not a final tax plan or a tax increase proposal.
  • Not an attack on current leadership, SMSD, or SEDC.
  • Not a replacement for professional legal or financial review.
  • Not a predetermined outcome or political campaign document.
  • Not an official statement of any public body.
Ethics Guardrails: This initiative is issue-focused and does not use City, SMSD, or SEDC resources. It does not imply official endorsement and complies with civic transparency guidelines.
