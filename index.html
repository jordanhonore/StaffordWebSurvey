Why this conversation matters now

Stafford is updating its 25-year Comprehensive Plan. Your Voice Matters! Should the plan include a realistic fiscal sustainability and revenue modernization framework (what does this mean?).

Official Context:

The City of Stafford’s Comprehensive Plan is intended to serve as a long-term blueprint for the community’s future. Comprehensive planning traditionally addresses land use, transportation, infrastructure, housing, schools, economic development, parks, open spaces, and related quality-of-life priorities.

Stafford’s current Comprehensive Plan was adopted in 2015. The consultant's cost for that planning effort was approximately $146,500 under the Freese and Nichols scope and compensation materials.

That investment should be respected. However, it also raises an important lesson for the next planning cycle: a 25-year vision is only as strong as the implementation and funding framework behind it.

Over the past decade, many of Stafford’s major public finance issues — public safety, infrastructure funding, restricted sales tax funds, public works needs, school finance pressures, economic development alignment, and total taxpayer burden — have continued to be debated separately. The result is that Stafford has had a long-term planning document, but not a fully integrated revenue blueprint to help carry the vision forward.

As Stafford moves through an updated Comprehensive Plan process, this is the right time for residents and local stakeholders to ask whether the plan should include a formal Fiscal Sustainability and Revenue Modernization Framework. The purpose of this survey is not to ask residents to approve a tax plan, endorse a political position, or support a predetermined outcome.

Can Stafford modernize its public revenue structure in a way that protects taxpayers, strengthens infrastructure, supports education, preserves restricted funds, and improves quality of life for the next generation?

Should Stafford study fiscal sustainability and revenue modernization as part of the Comprehensive Plan Update?

A complete long-range plan should align vision, infrastructure, public works, education, economic development, taxpayer impact, and funding capacity. This survey gives citizens, business owners, property owners, students, employees, parents, and consumers of Stafford businesses and services an opportunity to express whether they support including that study in the planning process.

Stafford should not invest in another 25-year planning process without also asking how the plan will be funded, implemented, measured, and sustained for the next generation.

The One Taxpayer Principle

While the government may allocate funds into distinct categories, residents and businesses perceive the financial burden as one cohesive community. School property taxes, county property taxes, sales taxes, Houston City College taxes, utility districts, franchise taxes, and other fees all impact the same taxpayer.

Every significant decision in public finance ought to consider: How does it affect the Stafford taxpayer overall, and what benefits are they gaining in return?

Four Study Areas

The One Taxpayer, One Stafford, One Future concept is built around four connected areas of study proposed for the Comprehensive Plan Update:

1. Sales Tax & Restricted Funds

Examine the current distributions, the motivations behind voter preferences, the sales tax dedicated to SEDC, the funds available for MST/property tax relief, and the potential legal avenues for realignment.

2. Public Works & Infrastructure

Look into specialized funding opportunities dedicated to improving drainage infrastructure, roads, pedestrian pathways, storm-water management, and routine maintenance activities.

3. Education & Youth Partnership

Investigate and develop legitimate collaborations within the city that focus on enhancing workforce preparedness, ensuring student achievement, improving safety in school zones, and elevating the overall quality of life for families.

4. Total Taxpayer Burden

Before finalizing any proposal, it is essential to thoroughly evaluate a model that promotes economic fairness in total tax burden from all property tax entities, VATRE, school district recapture values, revenue leakage, assessments, fees, and creative structures.

What We Mean by Fiscal Sustainability and Revenue Modernization

Fiscal sustainability means Stafford has a long-term financial structure that can realistically support the City’s future needs — including infrastructure, drainage, streets, public works, public facilities, economic development, education-related partnerships, and quality of life — without relying on short-term fixes, restricted funds being stretched beyond their purpose, or one-budget-cycle-at-a-time decision-making.

Revenue modernization means taking a fresh, comprehensive look at how public money is collected, allocated, restricted, and used across Stafford. It asks whether the current mix of sales tax, school property tax, economic development funds, public works funding, fees, assessments, and other revenue sources is still the best structure for the next 25 years.

Together, a Fiscal Sustainability and Revenue Modernization Framework would not approve a tax increase or adopt a final restructuring plan. It would simply create a disciplined study process to answer important questions before long-term decisions are made:

  • Are public revenues aligned with Stafford’s future priorities?
  • Are restricted funds being protected and used for their intended purpose?
  • Is there a fair and balanced way to support infrastructure, public safety, schools, public works, economic development, and quality of life?
  • Can Stafford better understand and manage the total burden on homeowners, commercial property owners, businesses, consumers, and other users of Stafford services?
  • Can the Comprehensive Plan include not only a vision for Stafford’s future, but also a realistic blueprint for how that future can be funded, implemented, and sustained?
In simple terms, this framework asks Stafford to connect the vision to the funding — before the next 25-year plan is completed.
