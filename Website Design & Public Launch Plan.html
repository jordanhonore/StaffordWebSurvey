Why this conversation matters now

Stafford is moving toward an update of its 25-year Comprehensive Plan. A vision for land use, infrastructure, housing, transportation, economic development, parks, and quality of life should also include a realistic fiscal sustainability and revenue modernization framework.

This initiative asks citizens and local leaders to support including that study in the Comprehensive Plan process.

Can Stafford modernize its public revenue structure in a way that protects taxpayers, strengthens infrastructure, supports education, preserves restricted funds, and improves quality of life for the next generation?

The One Taxpayer Principle

Government may divide funds into separate categories, but residents and businesses experience the cost as one community. School taxes, city revenue, sales tax, public works, fees, special districts, infrastructure, and school quality all affect the same taxpayer.

Every major public finance decision should ask: What is the total impact on the Stafford taxpayer, and what value is the taxpayer receiving in return?

Four study areas

Sales Tax & Restricted Funds

Map current allocations, voter intent, SEDC revenue, MST/property tax relief funds, and lawful realignment options.

Public Works & Infrastructure

Study dedicated funding for drainage, streets, sidewalks, stormwater, public facilities, and maintenance.

Education & Youth Partnership

Explore lawful city partnerships for workforce readiness, student success, school-area safety, and family quality of life.

Total Taxpayer Burden

Model property tax, VATRE, recapture, assessments, fees, and alternative structures before any final proposal.

What this is not

  • Not a final tax plan.
  • Not a proposal to immediately create a new tax.
  • Not an attack on current leadership, SMSD, or SEDC.
  • Not a replacement for professional legal or financial review.
